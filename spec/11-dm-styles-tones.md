# DM Styles, Philosophies, and Tone Calibration

Distilled from the DM-craft literature (Edwards, Laws, Baker, Harper, Cook, Shea, Mercer-discourse, OSR canon). Establishes the conceptual foundation for two engine knobs that must be **independent**: *tone* (what feeling the fiction evokes) and *DM style* (how the GM relates to fiction/players/rules).

For engine-specific implications, jump to [§8 Engine implications](#8-engine-implications).

---

## 1. TL;DR

- **Tone and style are two separable knobs.** Style governs *how* the GM relates to fiction, players, and rules; tone governs *what feeling* the fiction evokes.
- Foundational style taxonomy: **GNS / Threefold** (Edwards) — Gamism, Narrativism, Simulationism — refined into player-facing tribes by Robin Laws.
- Modern style camps: **OSR** ("rulings not rules," lethal sandbox); **PbtA / Story-Game** ("be a fan of the players," play-to-find-out); **Lazy DM** (improv-first, minimal prep); **Mercer-style** (high-production cinematic); the railroading–illusionism–participationism spectrum.
- Tones (heroic, gritty, horror, comedy, political, pulp, mystery, cozy, weird) are achieved through vocabulary, pacing, what you describe vs. omit, mechanical lethality, and what the dice are *for*.
- Style × tone interact strongly: OSR + horror = death-march dread; Mercer + heroic = cinematic epic; Lazy + cozy = conversational improv.
- Modern DMing is consent-forward: lines/veils, X-card, Stars & Wishes, session zero.
- **For the engine: tone shapes prompts and dials lethality; style shapes orchestration profile (which agents do what, how much improv vs. structure).**

## 2. DM philosophies / styles

### GNS / Threefold (Edwards)
Three creative agendas players bring to the table. **Gamism** = skill, competition, triumph via clever decisions. **Narrativism** = thematic, character-driven story built through choice. **Simulationism** = internal consistency, cause-and-effect fidelity to genre/world. DM serves whichever agenda dominates: Gamism wants fair challenges; Narrativism wants pressure on premises ("what do you do?"); Simulationism wants the world to obey its own logic regardless of drama.

### Robin Laws' Player Types
Power Gamer / Butt-Kicker / Tactician / Specialist / Method Actor / Storyteller / Casual. The DM's job is to recognize the table's mix and feed each their "kick" in the same session.

### Sandbox vs. Story / Play-to-Find-Out
- **Sandbox**: world is a place with situations, not a plot; GM prepares geography and factions, not arcs.
- **Story / authored arc**: GM has a planned narrative and steers toward it.
- **Play-to-find-out (PbtA)**: GM prepares fronts and threats but *refuses* to know the ending.

### Illusionism / Participationism / Railroading — a consent spectrum
- **Railroading**: GM forces the planned path regardless of player input (pejorative).
- **Illusionism**: GM manipulates outcomes invisibly while pretending to honor agency (the ethical fraught zone — *the trick is the lie*).
- **Participationism**: GM steers, but players know and consent.
- **Sandbox**: no GM Force.
The ethics turn on *whether players know*.

### OSR — "Rulings, not Rules" (Finch, 2008)
Four Zen moments: rulings not rules; player skill over character skill; heroic but not superheroes; "forget game balance." DM runs a deadly, exploration-first sandbox; rolls happen for *consequence*, not for "can my character do this?".

### Story Games / Narrativist (Baker, Crane, Harper)
Shared authorship; the GM is a fan of the PCs. MC agenda (AW): *Make the world seem real, make the PCs' lives not boring, play to find out what happens.* Principles: "Name everyone, make everyone human," "Ask provocative questions and build on the answers," "Be a fan of the players' characters."

### Lazy DM (Shea)
Eight-step minimal prep: strong start, potential scenes, secrets & clues, fantastic locations, NPCs, monsters, magic items, character ties. Prep evocative *seeds*, not scenes; trust improv and players to fill the middle. (See [`10-campaign-design.md`](10-campaign-design.md) §8 for full checklist.)

### Mercer-style / Critical Role
High-production cinematic DMing — distinctive NPC voices, music and lighting, miniature terrain, long-arc spotlight, theatrical pacing. Produces immersive serialized drama — and the "Mercer Effect" (unrealistic expectations on amateur DMs). For an engine: aspirational ceiling, not baseline.

### Old-school adversarial vs. modern collaborative
Early D&D framed the DM as referee and sometimes adversary; killing PCs was a feature. Modern DMing reframes the table as a *cooperative authorial group*. OSR is a deliberate partial revival of adversarial *neutrality* — not adversarial *malice*.

### Matt Colville / Running the Game
Pragmatic, verisimilitude-first DMing for new GMs. Build a world the players believe in; cater to player choices not GM plot; intelligent enemy tactics; explicitly inclusive tables. Produces grounded, action-rich D&D with strong worldbuilding pulse.

### The Angry GM
Opinionated, agency-centric craft writing. Core thesis: roleplay is *making choices* with *consequences* in a *consistent world*. Heavy on information management, encounter design, ruthless critique of "fanservice" DMing. Useful as a sanity check against pure improv.

### Safety / consent-forward DMing
Modern norm: explicit session zero with content lines (hard nos) and veils (off-screen yeses), an X-card for in-session escape, debrief tools like Stars & Wishes.

## 3. Tonal registers

### Heroic / Mythic (Forgotten Realms, Eberron, Critical Role)
- **Defining**: world-shaped stakes; PCs are exceptional; victory is satisfying and often earned with cool.
- **Techniques**: cinematic camera ("the dragon's shadow sweeps the valley"); name everything legendary; let players Win Cool; major NPC reactions to PC reputation.
- **Mechanical levers**: HP cushion, inspiration/hero point economies, climactic pacing, resurrection feasible.
- **Tone killers**: nihilism, casual PC death, scarcity prose in the climactic moment.

### Gritty / low-magic / dark fantasy (Warhammer, Mörk Borg, Shadow of the Demon Lord, Dark Sun)
- **Defining**: scarcity, body horror, moral grey; magic = corruption.
- **Techniques**: describe wounds and weather; name brands of cheap liquor not gemstones; NPCs lie, betray, are tired; sensory ugliness (smell, mud, rot).
- **Mechanical levers**: lethal HP, no resurrection, fumble tables, corruption/mutation tracks, expensive resources, hunger/exposure.
- **Tone killers**: Marvel quips, easy heroism, plot armor, found-money rewards, sanitized description.

### Horror (Call of Cthulhu, Trail of Cthulhu, Ten Candles, Dread, Alien, Mothership)
- **Defining**: powerlessness; the *unknown* > the seen; dread > shock.
- **Techniques**: long quiet beats then a single sharp detail; describe *implications*, not creatures (wet footprints, missing finger, song from the next room); use second person; refuse to fully answer; let silence sit; reserve full description for the moment players are committed.
- **Mechanical levers**: sanity/stress tracks, increasing failure probability (Dread's Jenga), permadeath, resource attrition (ammo, light, time).
- **Sub-registers**: cosmic (insignificance), psychological (the call is from inside), slasher (escape, not victory), corporate (Mothership/Alien — labor exploitation as horror).
- **Tone killers**: a quip, a power-fantasy moment, over-description of the monster, mechanical generosity, "rule of cool" saves.

### Comedic / picaresque (Paranoia, Honey Heist, Kobolds Ate My Baby)
- **Defining**: escalating absurdity; failure is funnier than success; permission to "yes-and."
- **Techniques**: comic timing — set up, beat, payoff; name minor NPCs absurdly; commit to bits; voice the bureaucracy; let consequences spiral; break the fourth wall *sparingly* and only when it lands.
- **Mechanical levers**: low character investment, fast generation, lethality played for laughs (Paranoia's six clones), random tables of escalating problems.
- **Tone killers**: tonal whiplash to seriousness without warning; player-on-player meanness; long stretches of mechanical resolution that kill comic rhythm.

### Political / intrigue (Burning Empires, Houses of the Blooded, Game-of-Thrones)
- **Defining**: information asymmetry; victory by *who knows what*; relationships as the battlefield.
- **Techniques**: NPCs with sharply different agendas; private notes, secret rolls, off-screen NPC maneuvering; slow-burn scenes; faction clocks; let player beliefs *force* conflict.
- **Mechanical levers**: faction/clock tracking, social conflict resolution (Duel of Wits), explicit info-economy systems.
- **Tone killers**: combat used to solve a social problem; full information disclosure; "the king reveals his plan" without earned access.

### Pulp / cinematic (Spirit of the Century, Savage Worlds, Indiana Jones)
- **Defining**: momentum, two-fisted prose, stunts, set-piece chases.
- **Techniques**: chained scenes (no downtime); name set-pieces ("the rope bridge over the chasm"); *describe motion* — verbs, present tense; let players narrate stunts; environmental hazards as flavor.
- **Mechanical levers**: bennies/fate points, aspects/tags for stunts, low-mid lethality, "yes and a complication" defaults.
- **Tone killers**: bookkeeping, long resolution math, paralysis-by-planning, grim consequences out of proportion.

### Mystery / investigative (Gumshoe, Trail of Cthulhu, Brindlewood Bay)
- **Defining**: the challenge is *interpretation*, not *finding*.
- **Techniques**: Three Clue Rule (≥3 clues per conclusion); distinguish *leads* (next location) from *evidence* (revelations); Gumshoe-style automatic core-clue discovery; Brindlewood-style player-interpretation of floating clues; name suspects and let players see them more than once.
- **Mechanical levers**: dedicated investigation skills; spend-based clue extras; "theorize" moves that let players *propose* solutions.
- **Tone killers**: a single point of failure in the clue chain; GM withholding to "make it harder."

### Slice-of-life / cozy (Wanderhome, Beach Episode, Ryuutama)
- **Defining**: low stakes, presence over plot, character & relationship focus, seasonal/transitional change.
- **Techniques**: ask sensory questions ("what color are the leaves where you wake up?"); honor silence; resolve disagreement through dialogue, not dice; let *change* (a season ends, an NPC departs) be the stakes.
- **Mechanical levers**: token economies (Wanderhome), travel and journey rules (Ryuutama), no-violence-as-default; "play to keep the world soft."
- **Tone killers**: combat-as-default, mechanical optimization discourse, cynicism, "but really what's the *plot*?"

### Surreal / weird (Numenera, Mage: the Ascension, Mothership)
- **Defining**: alienness handled without breaking immersion — the strange is *normal* to the world.
- **Techniques**: describe the impossible matter-of-factly; refuse to explain; use unfamiliar nouns ("the cypher hums"); ground weirdness in sensory specifics; let NPCs not understand it either.
- **Mechanical levers**: random weirdness tables, single-use bizarre items, scarcity of explanation, "ciphers" / one-shot tools.
- **Tone killers**: over-explanation, technobabble systematization, fan-wiki rationalization, modern-vernacular reactions ("that's bonkers").

## 4. The intersection — style × tone

Style and tone are orthogonal but interact strongly:

| Style × Tone | Result |
|---|---|
| **OSR + horror** | Death-march dread; solve your way out or die (Mothership, dungeon horror) |
| **OSR + heroic** | Earned zero-to-hero arcs where heroism is *earned* (B/X D&D) |
| **PbtA + horror** | Emotional psychological horror (Monsterhearts, The Between) |
| **PbtA + cozy** | Collaborative, low-stakes, player-authored softness (Wanderhome) |
| **Mercer + heroic** | Cinematic epic (Critical Role default) |
| **Mercer + horror** | Atmospheric horror theatre, risks underselling powerlessness |
| **Lazy + comedy** | Improv comedy unleashed |
| **Lazy + mystery** | Fragile unless Three Clue Rule is baked into prep templates |
| **Sandbox + political** | The player-faction sandbox (Burning Empires) |
| **Illusionist + heroic** | The classic "amateur Mercer" failure mode |

**Takeaway**: tone constrains *vocabulary, pacing, lethality*; style constrains *who authors what* and *when the DM intervenes*. Configure independently.

## 5. Safety / consent tooling

Modern consensus stack (Cook *Consent in Gaming*; Stavropoulos X-card; Quade Stars & Wishes; TTRPG Safety Toolkit):

- **Session zero** — agree on tone, content lines/veils, expectations for spotlight, table behavior.
- **Lines** = never appears. **Veils** = exists, happens off-screen ("fade to black"). Pre-agreed; may be private.
- **X-card** — in-session no-questions-asked retroactive veto on content.
- **Open-door policy** — anyone can leave any session, any time, no explanation.
- **Stars & Wishes** — end-of-session positive feedback: "stars" celebrate moments, "wishes" suggest forward-looking changes.
- **Script change / pause / rewind** — in-scene adjustment tools.
- **Consent checklists** (Cook) — pre-populated topic lists graded by comfort.

## 6. Common DM failure modes

- **Railroading invisibly** — illusionism players see through.
- **Quantum ogres** — pre-planned encounter "happens" regardless of choice; corrodes agency.
- **Death-by-clue** — one clue, one roll, mystery dies.
- **Tone whiplash** without warning (horror → quip, cozy → grimdark trauma).
- **DM as protagonist** — DMPC, DM monologues, players spectate.
- **Over-prep** — sunk-cost steering toward unused material.
- **Mercer-aping without budget** — performance pressure replaces presence.
- **Mechanical bailout** — fudging dice so often consequences stop mattering, or so rarely random death undermines investment.
- **Information hoarding** — withholding info "to make it harder" when the lever is *interpretation*, not *discovery*.
- **Ignoring the Casual Gamer / Storyteller** while feeding only Power Gamers and Tacticians.
- **Skipping safety tools** — fine until the night it isn't.

## 8. Engine implications

Direct translations into engine architecture (these expand [`02-tools-orchestration.md`](02-tools-orchestration.md), [`05-director.md`](05-director.md), [`08-cross-cutting.md`](08-cross-cutting.md)):

### 8.1 Tone and DM-style are independent configuration vectors
- **Tone** = output flavor (heroic / gritty / horror / comedy / political / pulp / mystery / cozy / weird). Persist per-campaign + per-scene override.
- **Style** = orchestration profile (OSR / PbtA / Lazy / Mercer / Sandbox-Sim). Persist per-campaign.
- Same tone can be served by very different styles, and vice versa.

### 8.2 Map tone to concrete prompt-level vocabulary biasing
Per-tone system-prompt fragments + lexical bias lists + style-of-speech exemplars in few-shot:
- *Heroic*: epic register, present-tense action, named legendary things, encourage second-person hero framing.
- *Gritty*: sensory ugliness, fatigue/scarcity nouns, blunt sentence lengths, withhold beauty.
- *Horror*: short paragraphs, implication over depiction, what *isn't* there, second-person, refuse-to-fully-answer behavior, never describe the monster in full until commitment.
- *Comedy*: comic rhythm, escalating absurd nouns, deadpan delivery of ridiculous things, payoff structures.
- *Political*: dense NPC interiority, private agendas, weighty pauses, "she watches you carefully" beats.
- *Pulp*: verb-forward present-tense motion, named set-pieces, complication-not-failure default.
- *Mystery*: privileged-information discipline, surface multiple clues per conclusion, accept player interpretations.
- *Cozy*: sensory questions to players, no combat-default, time-of-day and weather as story beats.
- *Weird*: matter-of-fact strangeness, unfamiliar nouns, refuse to systematize.

### 8.3 Tone modulates the rules engine, not just prose
Concrete dials owned by the deterministic engine:
- **Lethality multiplier** (PC HP, monster damage, save DCs).
- **Fail-forward weight** — gritty/horror = "fail = bad thing happens"; heroic/pulp = "fail = setback with momentum"; cozy = "fail = emotional beat."
- **Resurrection availability.**
- **Resource scarcity multiplier** (ammo, light, food, gold).
- **Sanity / Stress / Corruption tracks** — on/off; horror/gritty enables.
- **Complication-table flavor** tied to tone.

### 8.4 Tone biases generation tables
NPC trait pools, location atmosphere pools, encounter tables, treasure tables, weather tables all tone-tagged. A "tavern" in cozy returns warm bread and a sleeping cat; in gritty, sour ale and a thrown bottle; in horror, an empty room and a half-drunk mug still warm.

### 8.5 DM-style as the orchestration profile
Each style = a distinct agent system prompt + tool-policy bundle:
- *Lazy*: low-prep, improv-first, generate-on-demand, accept ambiguity, reuse content aggressively.
- *OSR*: rulings-not-rules — adjudicate from fiction not stat block when in doubt; give players *information* to make smart calls, then let consequence fall.
- *PbtA / Story-game*: explicit MC agenda baked in ("be a fan of the players, ask provocative questions, play to find out, never pre-author outcomes"); use moves not arbitrary calls.
- *Mercer*: distinct NPC voices (multimodal handoff — see [`09-multimodality.md`](09-multimodality.md)), recurring NPCs with arcs, spotlight rotation, cinematic combat description.
- *Sandbox-Sim*: pre-generate factions and locations; world reacts logically; refuses to bend to dramatic convenience.

### 8.6 Player-type recognition (Laws)
Track per-PC how the player engages — combat-seek, optimization-seek, character-voice-seek, narrative-seek, lurker — and bias spotlight allocation and prompt offers accordingly. DM agent should *budget* attention across types in a session. Director's spotlight tracker (see [`05-director.md`](05-director.md)) gets a player-type dimension.

### 8.7 Three Clue Rule as a generation invariant
Any "investigation" scene generated must include ≥3 clue paths to each load-bearing conclusion. Distinguish *leads* from *evidence*. Make this a generator validator, not a hope. (Also flagged in [`10-campaign-design.md`](10-campaign-design.md).)

### 8.8 Safety tooling as first-class engine features
- Session-zero wizard (lines, veils, tone choice, Cook-style content checklist).
- Persistent per-player content filter applied at generation *and* at output (two-layer: don't write it; if written, redact and regenerate).
- In-session `/x-card` slash command that pauses, retroactively veils the last beat, continues.
- Built-in Stars & Wishes prompt at session end, persisted as design feedback.
- "Pause / fade-to-black / rewind" affordances.

### 8.9 Railroading–participationism dial explicit and visible
Slider from "pure sandbox (engine never bends outcomes)" → "participationist (engine may bend with player consent and announces when)" → **off-limits illusionist mode (don't ship the dishonest one)**. Visible state is the ethical fix; the engine *tells* the players when the dial moves.

### 8.10 Tone-violation guardrails
A "tone keeper" pass on DM output checks for: quips in horror; modern slang in mythic; sanitized description in gritty; gore in cozy; combat-defaulting in cozy/political. Either re-roll the response or annotate, gated by a "strictness" setting.

### 8.11 Fail-forward as a routed-choice, not a global setting
When a roll fails, the DM agent picks from a tone-shaped menu:
- *Hard fail* (gritty/horror default)
- *Success with cost* (PbtA/pulp default)
- *Succeed-but* (cozy default — emotional rather than physical cost)
- *Cascade complication* (sandbox-sim default — the world advances)

### 8.12 "Tone diary" for long-term calibration
Log per-scene: operating tone, deviations the model produced, player reactions (explicit or inferred). Use to (a) detect drift, (b) feed Stars & Wishes summarization, (c) calibrate future scenes. This is the engine's equivalent of a DM's between-session reflection — and the highest-leverage long-term quality lever.
