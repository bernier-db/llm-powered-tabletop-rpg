# TTRPG Campaign & Scenario Design — Best Practices

Distilled from the practitioner literature (Alexandrian, Sly Flourish, PbtA/BitD designers, OSR canon, Angry GM, Robin Laws). Provides the craft foundation the Director, scenario generator, and DM agent should encode.

For the engine-specific implications, jump to [§11 Engine implications](#11-engine-implications).

---

## 1. TL;DR

- **Prep situations, not plots.** OSR, PbtA, and FitD converge here: design dynamic situations (factions, locations, NPCs with goals, ticking clocks) and let player choices generate the story (Alexandrian; Apocalypse World; Sly Flourish).
- **Redundancy beats single-thread plotting.** The Three Clue Rule and node-based design make scenarios robust to player creativity and missed rolls (Alexandrian).
- **Strong starts and modular scenes.** Lazy DM model: prep 5–10 secrets, a strong open, fantastic locations; let scenes self-assemble at the table.
- **Clocks are the killer mechanic for dynamism.** Fronts (PbtA) and faction/project clocks (BitD) give the world impulse independent of the players.
- **NPCs: one trait, one want, one secret, one tic.** Minimal but distinctive (Colville; Sly Flourish).
- **Encounters need objectives beyond "win the fight"** — terrain, hostages, ticking clocks, escape conditions.
- **Foreshadow with distance and density** — multiple hooks across sessions; payoff feels earned only after a lag.
- **The "quantum ogre" is the canonical failure mode** of illusionism: pretending to honor choice while invalidating it.

## 2. Campaign-level structure

### Arc archetypes
- **One-shot / single session**: classic 5-Room Dungeon (Johnn Four) or a single Score (BitD).
- **Mini-arc (4–8 sessions)**: a single Front (PbtA) or a node-based mystery (Alexandrian). **Good default unit for our engine.**
- **Adventure-path / epic (level 1–20)**: serial chapters threaded by a Big Bad. Paizo APs are the canonical example; APs accept some railroading because the macro-arc is the product.

### Seeding long threats early
- **Fronts (PbtA, Dungeon World)** — each major threat is a Front with a Dark Future (what happens if PCs ignore it) and a countdown clock. Tick during downtime.
- **Faction clocks (BitD)** — each faction has long-term goal clocks and short-term project clocks; GM rolls Tier dice on downtime to tick. The world is alive, not waiting.

### Handling derailment — three doctrines
1. **"Play to find out" (Dungeon World agenda)** — never plan a storyline; moves rewrite it anyway.
2. **Inverted Three Clue Rule (Alexandrian)** — if players have any three clues, they'll reach *some* conclusion; you don't need to control which.
3. **Quantum content, NOT quantum choices (Colville/Shea)** — fine to reuse a prepped scene anywhere; *not* fine to invalidate a deliberate player choice.

## 3. Scenario structure templates

| Template | Source | Shape | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5-Room Dungeon** | Johnn Four | Entrance/Guardian → Puzzle/RP → Trick/Setback → Climax → Reward/Twist | One-shots, side quests, sub-locations |
| **3-Act / dramatic question** | Angry GM | Hook → rising tension → climax that answers a dramatic question | Single sessions with clear "will they?" hook |
| **Node-based design** | Alexandrian | Locations/people/clues as nodes; each has 3+ connections | Mysteries, investigations, sandboxes |
| **Score** | BitD | Engagement roll → flashbacks → escalating clocks → payoff/entanglements | Heists, missions, high-agency single-session |
| **Front** | Apocalypse / Dungeon World | Linked threats with stakes questions + countdown clocks | Background threats running parallel to PC action |
| **Hexcrawl** | OSR | Keyed hexes + random encounters + travel procedures | Open-world sandbox campaigns |
| **Lazy adventure** | Sly Flourish | Strong start + scenes + 7–10 secrets + locations + NPCs + monsters + treasure | DM-light prep for ongoing campaigns |

### Branching vs. linear
Linear *inside* a scene/room is fine; *between* scenes there should always be 2+ ways forward. Node-based natively encodes this redundancy.

### What to prep vs. improvise (Shea)
Prep what's hard to improvise (evocative locations, NPC names, monster lists, magic items, a strong open); improvise what comes naturally (transitions, minor NPCs, dialogue). PbtA / BW take this further: prep the *situation pressure* (BITs, fronts), let *all* outcomes emerge from play.

## 4. NPC craft

The most-cited formula across Colville, Sly Flourish, Nerds on Earth:

> **Name + one obvious mannerism + one want + one secret.**

Concrete techniques:
- **Caricatural single quirk** — accent, tic, repeated phrase, prop they always touch. Lean on it.
- **Derive personality from stats** (Colville) — high INT uses fancy vocab; high DEX juggles fruit as they talk. Free flavor from mechanics.
- **Wants drive interaction; secrets drive recurrence.** The want gives the NPC a stance in scene; the secret gives the GM ammo for next scene.
- **Recurring antagonists need exits.** Give the villain reliable escape routes early; let them survive defeats to become hated.
- **Burning Wheel BITs** (Belief, Instinct, Trait) — NPCs become self-driving in the GM's head.
- **"Name every person"** (Dungeon World principle) — a named NPC is instantly more real than "the guard."

See [`04-npc-memory.md`](04-npc-memory.md) for the memory-side complement and [`06-generation.md`](06-generation.md) for the NPC generation pipeline.

## 5. Faction & clock design

Why clocks dominate modern design: they externalize world momentum. Things happen whether or not PCs act. Three core flavors:

- **Countdown clock (PbtA, 6-segment)** — quarter-hour ticks for slow build, 5-minute ticks for urgent endgame. Both *descriptive* and *prescriptive* — if the trigger event happens, the clock jumps to that tick.
- **Progress clock (BitD, 4/6/8 segment)** — 4 = obstacle, 6 = complicated, 8 = daunting. Filled by player actions or as consequences of partial successes.
- **Faction clocks (BitD)** — each faction has long-term goal clocks; GM rolls Tier dice on downtime to tick.

### Multi-faction sustain
1. **Goal triangulation** — each faction wants something *another faction has or blocks*. Conflict is automatic.
2. **Tier asymmetry** — mix tiers so PCs can play sides off each other.
3. **Visible scoreboard** — show players the clocks (or paraphrase). Dramatic irony beats secret simulation.

### Bonus: Burning Wheel BITs as player-side fronts
Player Beliefs are GM-mandated challenges — player-side "fronts" exist, balancing the antagonist clocks.

## 6. Foreshadowing & payoff

Techniques distilled from screenwriting tradition that GMs borrow:

- **Plant ≥3 times, harvest once.** A single hint reads as random; three hints across sessions read as inevitability.
- **Distance is what makes the payoff feel earned.** A gun introduced and fired in the same scene isn't Chekhov's gun — it's a plot point.
- **Plant ambient, harvest specific.** Drop the bounty hunter's emblem on three random NPCs over six sessions; reveal him by name in session 10.
- **Item-as-callback** — leave undefined objects/names in early prep; bind them to later events retroactively.
- **Visible long lead.** For epic stakes ("the moon will fall"), make it visible in fiction well before mechanically relevant. Players who see foreshadowing they didn't decode feel respected, not tricked.

## 7. Encounter design

### Combat
- **Objectives beyond HP** — hostage to save, ritual to interrupt, escape clock, collapsing terrain.
- **Terrain as a fifth combatant** — cover, obstacles, hazards. Five goblins on a road ≠ five goblins on lily pads.
- **Best encounters blend 2–3 modes** (combat + social + exploration in one scene).

### Social
- **Stakes question first** — what does each side want; cost of failure?
- **Compels (FATE)** — GM proposes a complication tied to a character aspect; player accepts (and gets fate point) or buys out.

### Exploration
- **Random encounter tables aren't filler** — OSR doctrine: encounter tables encode the *ecology* of a region.
- **Tension Pool (Angry GM)** — add dice to a pool whenever players burn time; roll at thresholds to trigger complications. Mechanizes "the cost of dawdling."

### Difficulty calibration
- Pathfinder's XP-budget approach is the cleanest numerical model.
- D&D 5e CR is famously loose.
- PbtA/BitD intentionally don't calibrate (fiction-first).
- **For an LLM engine, calibrated math is easier to verify than narrative pressure.**

## 8. Session-level pacing — Lazy DM 8 steps (Shea)

The single most-cited modern session-prep checklist:

1. Review the characters
2. Create a **strong start** (cold open — a punch, mystery, discovery)
3. Outline potential scenes (modular, not sequential)
4. Define 7–10 **secrets and clues** — context-free, droppable anywhere
5. Develop fantastic locations
6. Outline important NPCs (name + goal + quirk)
7. Choose relevant monsters
8. Select magic items / rewards

### Scene framing (Robin Laws, *Hamlet's Hit Points*)
Classify every beat as *hope* or *fear* (or *up*/*down*) and watch the rhythm. A scene should change the emotional needle.

### When to cut
Cut when the scene's dramatic question has been answered, or when momentum drops. PbtA: cut to ask "what do you do?" the moment uncertainty re-enters.

### End strong
Cliffhanger, revelation, or visible new threat that promotes a clock — close one beat, open another.

## 9. Player agency vs. authored story — the spectrum

| Pole | Label | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hard railroad | "Adventure path" | Paizo APs (acknowledged) |
| Soft railroad / illusionism | "Quantum ogre" | Same encounter wherever you go |
| Participationism | Players know there's a plot and opt in | Many published adventures |
| Node-based / sandbox | Situation prepped, plot emerges | Alexandrian; OSR |
| Pure improv | "Play to find out" | PbtA, BitD |

### The quantum ogre critique
The problem isn't *reusing prep*, it's *invalidating informed choice*. If players smell the ogre behind door A and pick door B, putting the ogre behind door B is a betrayal. **Reframe: prep content, not fate.** The same ogre can show up later in a different meaningful context.

### Best balance heuristic
Prep what you want to *be possible*; never prep what you want to *be inevitable*. Inevitability lives in fronts/clocks, not in scenes.

## 10. Common failure modes

- **Overplanning a single path** — single-thread mysteries die when one clue is missed. Fix: Three Clue Rule.
- **Quantum ogre / fake choices** — players smell it. Fix: prep nodes; honor recon.
- **Info-gating behind one skill check** — the dice will eat your scenario. Fix: information findable multiple ways.
- **"My story" syndrome** — GM has a novel in mind; players are extras. Fix: convert plot to fronts + situations.
- **No strong start** — sessions begin with shopping. Fix: cold open.
- **Combat without objective** — HP-attrition slugfests. Fix: terrain + objective + clock.
- **NPC overload with no quirks** — players can't tell them apart. Fix: one mannerism per NPC, ruthlessly.
- **Static world** — if PCs do nothing, nothing happens. Fix: faction & front clocks tick anyway.
- **Reward miscalibration** — treasure unrelated to character arcs. Fix: pre-select bespoke magic items.
- **No payoff for foreshadowing** — plants without harvest. Fix: maintain a "foreshadow ledger" and discharge it on schedule.

## 11. Engine implications

Direct translations into engine architecture (these expand the [`05-director.md`](05-director.md) and [`06-generation.md`](06-generation.md) designs):

1. **Scenario authoring schema = nodes, not chapters.** Persist scenarios as `{nodes, edges, clues, npcs, locations}`. Each node holds 3+ outbound clue-edges. The DM agent retrieves nodes by relevance, not sequence. Maps to the existing relationships table in [`01-storage.md`](01-storage.md).

2. **"Foreshadow queue" as a first-class table.** Plants need explicit storage with `planted_at_session`, `target_payoff_session_min`, `harvested` flags. The Director prioritizes spending a plant whose target window has arrived; if a plant rots unspent across N sessions, surface to the DM for retconning into the current scene.

3. **Fronts/clocks as a sim loop.** Implement Apocalypse-World-style Fronts as `{name, dark_future, stakes_questions[], clocks[]}`. Every downtime tick or session boundary, run a "world tick" pass: roll faction Tier dice (BitD) to advance 1–2 clocks. Gives the world momentum without DM-agent intervention.

4. **Scene-brief format = Lazy DM steps 2–7 instantiated.** Each scene brief the Director hands the DM agent should contain: strong-start hook, location with 3 sensory details, NPC(s) with `{name, want, quirk, secret}`, available secrets/clues (7–10 pool, none scene-locked), optional encounter (with objective, not just monsters).

5. **NPC schema enforces minimalism.** Hard-cap at: `name, one_mannerism, one_want, one_secret, voice_note`. Resist NPC bloat — stat-derived flavor + one tic outperforms biography.

6. **Director's prime directive: prep situations, not paths.** When generating campaign content, emit Fronts + nodes + NPCs with goals, never a "next scene." The DM agent decides at runtime which node fires.

7. **Quantum-ogre guardrail.** Add an explicit invariant: once the engine commits a fact to fiction (clue, location, NPC presence), it cannot be retconned away in response to player choice. Foreshadow queue can *add* content, never *invalidate* a player-deduced fact. Log every commitment to a `world_facts` ledger the DM agent reads before generating.

8. **Three-Clue invariant for mystery generation.** Any scenario node that depends on the players reaching a conclusion must have ≥3 clue-edges leading to it from different nodes. Validate at authoring time.

9. **Tension pool / clock-advance as system mechanics.** The deterministic rules engine should own clock advancement (failed rolls fill clocks BitD-style; time-burning actions tick the tension pool). Don't leave clock movement to the LLM — determinism win.

10. **Session-end hook = strong end + clock report.** Every session-close ritual: (a) advance world clocks visibly, (b) plant at least one new foreshadowing seed, (c) leave players on a beat that opens a new scene-question. Bakes hope/fear oscillation into cadence.
